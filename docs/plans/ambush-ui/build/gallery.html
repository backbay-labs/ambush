Wave 2 · the drawn work

A shift-shaped queue where a human decision is typed, signed and recorded.

Perch is Ambush’s operator console: the Buzz desktop app, re-skinned and cut by a third. Five surfaces were drawn as standalone HTML you can open by double-clicking — not wireframes with a dark background, but working pages on the shipping token file, carrying the canonical demo fixture’s real numbers. This page presents them, renders the palette as swatches with its measured contrast, and shows the ramps and chart primitives the surfaces are built from.

The one thing to understand before looking: Perch never authorizes. A decision is two legs — a signed intent card published to the relay, and a separate POST to the daemon, which re-derives authority from scratch. Everything drawn here is downstream of that, and every badge on every card says what it can actually prove.

One

The five drawn surfaces

Each opens by double-clicking — no build step, no network, no CDN, no external font. Each carries its own spec-notes panel with the citations behind every decision, and each draws its empty, degraded and adversarial states rather than only its happy one.

Reading the miniatures. The posters above are drawn for this page in the same tokens as the prototypes — they are honest about layout and colour and deliberately simplified about density. The real pages carry roughly ten times the content. Open them.

Why every name is --perch-*. Buzz’s ThemeProvider writes 38 shadcn variables — --background, --card, --foreground, --muted-foreground, --popover, --ring, --border among them — inline on the root element on every theme change and again on the cached-boot path before first paint. An inline declaration outranks every normal-priority stylesheet rule, so a surface authored against those names repaints with whichever Buzz syntax theme the operator last picked. The namespace is not hygiene; it is the difference between the palette shipping and the palette silently not existing. tokens/perch-tokens.test.mjs now asserts both halves of that contract: that every bare name in the drawings has a translation row, and that the translation was actually applied.

Three

The four ramps

Every ramp is closed — a fixed set of values with no “other”. That is deliberate: an open ramp is how a console ends up inventing a state the runtime does not have, and then rendering it as if the runtime said so.

Severity

Four steps. The rail carries the signal; the word is confirmation, not the channel. Both themes clear 4.5:1 on all five surfaces.

LOWinformational; never pages
MEDIUMenters queue 2, reviewed on shift
HIGHhuman_gate_severity — the gate opens here
CRITICALwake class 1; the phone rings

Verification tier

A tier and the chain it checked — never a checkmark, never the word “verified”. Four of the seven card types can never rise above 0.

tier 0 · UNATTESTEDfinding, escalation, hold and containment — no signature exists
tier 0 · BY DESIGNunder a partition contingency; the absence is the correct state
tier 1 · Ed25519rollback only — signed and bound to this subject
tier 2 · not reachableneeds a configured signing identity and a per-issuer seq store

Governance posture

Exactly four values, closed. It is a partition state, not a health score, and the strip renders how stale the reading is beside it.

Healthycommittee reachable, quorum intact
Degradedreachable, below preferred quorum
Partitionedcontingency leases, 5 min, not 60 s
Healingrejoining; receipts still carry the gap

Confidence

Five hollow ticks, filled to the detector’s own number. Never a percentage on its own, and never rounded up into a claim the detector did not make.

 0.90peak_confidence on the crossing deposit
 0.55a single command_and_control finding
 0.31below what any threat-class lane escalates on alone
 —no confidence carried; the absence is rendered

The three hues are decoration, not classification. The pillar borders (substrate, authority, evidence) measure 1.31–1.58:1 against a card. They were kept because they are the reference artwork’s own values, and reclassified: the 2.5 px top rail at 8.6–10.6:1 is the classification channel, and no card’s meaning may depend on its border.

Four

The chart primitives

Six components, hand-authored SVG, no charting library in the dependency tree. They exist because the console must never disagree with swarmctl about a number: the runtime snapshot is authoritative, anything the console computes carries a marker naming the function that computed it, and a disagreement snaps visibly with a reason row rather than resolving quietly in the console’s favour.

Five

The type ramp

Nine steps, all rem-derived from Buzz’s virtual typography rem, so every one of them rides the user’s font-size preference and ⌘+/− zoom. Sizes below are at the default 16 px rem. A CI guard fails the build on any arbitrary text-size literal — px or rem.

--perch-text-xl20px Surface titles
--perch-text-lg18px Section headings
--perch-text-base16px The action line of a hold — the one string that must read across a room
--perch-text-sm14px Conversation and queue-row body. 43.4% of rendered nodes sit at or above this.
--perch-text-xs12px Secondary rows: scope, host, expiry
--perch-text-eyebrow12px Slot labels
--perch-text-2xs11px Meta text, count badges, key hints — and nothing a decision depends on
--perch-text-badge10px TIER 0 · UNATTESTED
--perch-text-3xs8px Chart tick labels only. Zero product strings render at this size.

The type census, measured. A headless pass over the drawn surfaces in both themes counted 594 visible text nodes: 27.6% at 11 px, 29.0% at 12 px, 40.4% at 14 px, 3.0% at 16 px or above — and zero at 8 px. The first revision of this set put up to 39% of a wall screen at 8 px; that is the single largest change between revisions, and it is the one worth checking first when you open the pages.

The honest summary. The wire contract, the fixture, the token package, the relay patches and the refactor arithmetic are verified — they run, from the paths they are committed at, and reproduce their numbers. The Rust crates, the CI gates’ wiring and every route the backend bill adds are proposed: they are specified to the file level and priced, and none of them exists yet. Nothing in this set has been compiled into either repository.
