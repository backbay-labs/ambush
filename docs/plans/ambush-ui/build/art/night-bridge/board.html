Bridge

Perch · art direction

A darkened bridge keeps a tired person accurate for eight hours by doing two things: it paints the panel a colour that does not glare, and it burns as few lamps as it can. The panel is warm graphite and it is not lit. The night outside the glass is colder and darker than anything on it. Two lanterns burn, and colour never lands on a word.

Chroma in this system is light falling on a surface, never ink. Every glyph is bone on graphite, so the whole console survives colour-blindness, a bad monitor and a greyscale print by construction — and the two lit things on the screen are the only two things that can hurt you.

C

The system

Two lanterns, a nine-step warm graphite ramp, and one typeface family drawn at two widths.

The neutral ramp — the console

nightfieldpanellit lineruledimbodybone

Warm graphite: painted marine panel, not a blue-grey console. The ground it floats on — --night — is the only cold value in the file and the darkest, and that is the whole temperature split. It costs nothing from the chromatic budget because both halves are neutrals, which is why it survives into light mode where the deck is a grey-painted steel and the plates are pale stone.

--rule is a structural token and is never text. Every glyph on this board is --dim or lighter.

Both are derived rather than picked: a signal-lantern dominant wavelength mixed toward CIE Illuminant A — the incandescent source marine lanterns are specified against — and then added to the graphite panel, because what you see at night is not the lamp, it is the panel under the lamp. §D has the arithmetic.

Severity gets no lantern. Neither does policy, provenance, confidence, "no undo mapped" or a CRITICAL row. Each of those already carries a word, a shape and a position, and a lamp on a queue row would be a lamp you learn to stop reading.

Barlow is a transport-signage grotesque, drawn for the flat, wide, low-contrast lettering on vehicles and public infrastructure — and it ships a genuine condensed cut in the same family, which is exactly what an instrument panel does: the placard and the silkscreen come off one drawing at two widths. Inter is what I would reach for by default on any dark UI and it has no condensed cut with this character.

One rule makes the pane checkable: mono means the machine's own bytes; Barlow means English. Ids, keys, times, thresholds, strengths, rule names and the daemon's verbatim strings are mono, and nothing else is. Plex Mono has the flat-topped 1, the unambiguous 0 and tabular figures that let a 64-hex key and a six-decimal concentration sit in a column without wobbling. Sizes are seven rem tokens — 3.5 / 2.375 / 1.375 / 1.0625 / .9375 / .8125 / .6875 rem — with no px anywhere in the file, so the app's zoom contract holds.

Where the two lanterns came from

The previous pass of this direction assigned blue = observation, violet = policy, amber = caution, red = danger, and then nudged the hexes until they cleared a fence. That is the standard semantic four with different digits. This is the derivation that replaced it.

The arithmetic, and what it refused to give me

Take the cold light first The dark-adapted eye peaks at 507 nm — that is the physical fact night vision runs on. Rendered on graphite at any usable luminance it produces #009368, #00A877, #00BE86 — ΔE00 1.10 to 4.98 from #059669 and #10b981. The rod peak is emerald. Physics does not excuse a banned look.
